## What it says

**The library-built grid is not a replacement for the frozen tables as
it stands.** On the one plan tried it names the same number of defects,
touches four fewer, and costs about 1.7 times the cells and the
reading. A third of it (388 cells) is "cannot happen here": crossing
every thing the slice does with every entry of its kind asks, for
instance, twelve questions about comparing and sorting text of a fixed
message that is never compared or sorted.

**The two methods name different defects.** Six are named by both, four
only by the frozen tables, four only by the grid: fourteen of thirty
between them.

- *Only the frozen tables:* two that come from the provider's
  documentation (how much history the first update covers, and the
  request's default window), one malformed value and one race on first
  use. The frozen method has a dossier step and a table of every value
  against every way it can be wrong; the grid has neither. Five of the
  eight fillers read the provider's documentation and three did not.
- *Only the grid:* the wrong institution accepted under a card's name,
  an empty set shown as connected, a damaged file at start, and a key
  of the wrong length. These came from the library's entries on
  reference data, on what is received, on loading at start and on
  secrets.
- *Lost entirely by the grid (part before, nothing now):* four defects
  about one malformed setting or one malformed field of a reply. The
  library asks about input in general; it does not put each setting and
  each field against each way of being wrong, as the frozen method's
  fourth table does.

**Both grids stop at about a third.** Two different ways of laying out
a completeness grid each name 10 of 30, and 14 together. Of the 16
neither names, the judge puts between three and five down to decisions
the plan made wrongly; the rest are a rule or wording of the product, a
defect of the code, or a case the plan was silent on that neither grid
asked about in this product's terms. More cells is not what is
missing.

## Cost

About 132,000 tokens to list the slice's actions, 1,210,000 for the
eight fillers and 790,000 for the three matcher runs (each about three
times a run on the frozen tables, the grid being eight times the
text).

## Limits

- One plan, one listing of actions, one filling. The run-to-run spread
  of the listing (98 items) and of the filling is not measured; a
  difference of two or three defects is within what one run shows, and
  "named" is level on that reading, "named or part" four apart.
- The matcher is less steady on the larger grid (21 of 30 on the exact
  class), mostly between W and P where a decided cell and a silent one
  both sit near the defect.
- Eight fillers each saw one part of the grid and the whole plan; a
  point one of them found (the plan's two rules for a leftover connect
  contradict each other) appears in several parts under different
  cells.
- The frozen tables were built by one agent that read the provider's
  documentation throughout; the fillers were allowed to and five did.
- The bar was proposed by the session and not confirmed by the owner
  before the result was read.
